Molecular basis of diversity in fishes

Teleosts are the most species-rich group of vertebrates, having adapted to environments from seawater to freshwater and diversified rapidly. To understand the evolutionary mechanisms behind their phenotypic diversity, I use ricefishes (Adrianichthyidae), a family that has diversified remarkably across East and Southeast Asia, as the main model, and apply genomic and genetic analyses to identify the responsible genomic regions and genes. State-of-the-art genome editing is then used to test the function of the molecules and regions identified. The same approaches are also being extended to a wider range of aquatic animals, both to study evolution over broader timescales and to support breeding of aquaculture species.

01Diversification of sexual dimorphism under sexual selection

Traits that differ markedly between the sexes are found across a wide range of organisms, and the traits involved diversify rapidly. They are thought to have evolved under sexual selection, which acts through competition over reproduction such as mate choice and male–male competition. For the diverse sexually dimorphic traits seen in ricefishes, we search for the controlling genes by genomic analysis and combine genome editing with behavioral assays to examine how phenotypic evolution relates to sexual selection.

02Karyotype evolution, speciation and reproductive isolation

Differences between species in chromosome number and shape (karyotype) have long been documented by classical cytogenetics, yet their evolutionary significance remains largely unresolved. Changes in karyotype are thought to cause reproductive isolation by disrupting normal cell division in hybrids, and thus to drive speciation, but direct tests are scarce. Using the karyotypic diversity within ricefishes as a model, we combine genomic analysis with experiments to reveal how chromosomal rearrangements arise and how they contribute to reproductive isolation and speciation.

03Sex chromosome turnover and the diversity of sex-determination systems

Recent work has shown that sex-determining genes and sex chromosomes turn over repeatedly in teleosts, even among closely related species. Using ricefishes as a model, we study how new sex-determining genes are acquired, how sex chromosomes differentiate, and how differences in sex-determination systems affect hybridization and speciation. We also extend the same analyses to a range of teleosts, including commercially important species, to identify the evolutionary forces that generate the diversity of sex chromosomes.

04Advancing genetic modification technologies in fish

We have worked on genetic modification technologies, including the early adoption of genome editing in medaka. Alongside continued development of more efficient methods, such as knock-in with the CRISPR/Cas system and transposon-mediated transgenesis, we build research resources for broad use in basic research, for example by introducing neuroscience tools. We also actively transfer these technologies to other species, aiming to establish a technical basis for analyzing gene function across diverse species.

05Genetic basis of aquaculture traits through evolutionary genomics

The genomic analysis methods advancing in evolutionary biology can also be applied to breeding commercially important aquatic species, aquaculture fishes in particular. We identify genes and variants underlying useful traits such as sex control and growth, and build the analytical resources needed to advance the breeding of improved strains.
